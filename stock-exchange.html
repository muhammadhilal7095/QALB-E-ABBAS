Learn to read trading charts

The five chart skills every investor should understand before buying a share.

Candlesticks

Each candle shows the open, high, low and close for one day, green when the price rose, red when it fell.

Moving averages

A smoothed line of recent prices. Price above the average often signals an uptrend; below it, weakness.

Support & resistance

Price levels where buying (support) or selling (resistance) has repeatedly stopped the move.

Volume

How many shares traded. Big moves on high volume are usually more meaningful than quiet ones.

Trends & trend lines

Higher highs and higher lows make an uptrend. A trend line helps you see when it breaks.

Stock exchange investing: FAQs

How do I start investing in US and UK shares?

Open an account with a broker regulated in the US (SEC and FINRA) or the UK (FCA) that accepts clients from your country. You'll need a passport or ID, proof of address and a bank account in your name. Your shares are held in your name by the broker's custodian. We guide you through each step and help you choose a broker.

Can I invest if I don't live in the US or UK?

Often, yes. Many international brokers accept clients from other countries, and non-US investors usually complete a W-8BEN tax form. Availability, fees and rules depend on where you live, so we check which regulated brokers fit your situation.

Which is better for beginners: single shares or ETFs?

For most beginners, low-cost index ETFs are the simpler start: one ETF can hold hundreds of companies, such as the whole S&P 500 or FTSE 100, which spreads your risk. Single shares can be added later once you understand how to research a company.

How much money do I need to start?

You can start learning and investing with a modest amount. Many brokers and mutual funds have low minimums, and some US and UK brokers allow fractional shares. More important than the amount is investing only money you can leave invested for several years and keeping a separate emergency fund.

Do you give buy and sell tips?

No. We don't sell "hot tips" or trading signals. We teach you how to research companies, build a diversified portfolio and manage risk, and we help you create a plan that fits your goals. Managed portfolio clients receive a written agreement explaining exactly how their money is managed.

Is stock market investing halal?

Many scholars consider investing in Shariah-compliant shares permissible. There are Shariah-screened indices and ETFs that cover US, UK and global shares. We help you build a portfolio from Shariah-compliant options; for religious rulings, please consult a qualified scholar.

Can I lose money in the stock market?

Yes. Share prices go up and down, and you may get back less than you invest. Past performance is not a guide to future returns. Diversification, a long-term approach and investing only what you can afford reduce the risk, but they don't remove it. Treat anyone who promises guaranteed profits with caution.

Do you manage portfolios for clients?

Yes, managed portfolios are available for clients who prefer us to handle day-to-day investment decisions. This is provided only under the required regulatory permission, by us or by a licensed partner, with full regulatory details and a written agreement before you invest. Your investments stay in an account in your own name.

What taxes apply to stock market profits?

It depends on where you live and where you invest. US shares usually have withholding tax on dividends for non-US investors (often reduced with a W-8BEN), and UK residents may use a Stocks and Shares ISA. We explain the general rules; for personal tax advice, speak to a tax adviser.

How do I book a stock market consultation?

Book online, choose a time that suits you and pay securely. In the session we review your goals, budget and risk level, and recommend whether to start with a course, open an account or explore a managed portfolio.
